Chinese-mythology-inspired rhythm-action roguelike. Rabbit martial artist protagonist in a 朝廷迷宫 (imperial court labyrinth). Every attack is beat-quantized.

**Core Pillars:**
1. Rhythm IS Combat — beat quantization is the combat OS, not optional
2. Wuxia Feel First — rhythm layered onto solid action feel, never replaces it
3. Chinese Cultural Authenticity — setting is a world-building principle, not a skin
4. Build Depth Over Stat Inflation — perks change rhythm rules, not just numbers

**Anti-Pillars (explicit NOT):**
- NOT narrative-driven — "story is the player's skill mastery journey"
- NOT track rhythm game (action game with rhythm dimension)
- NOT multiplayer at launch
- NOT bypass-rhythm invincibility moves

**Narrative priority in MDA:** Ranked 6th of 6 active aesthetics. Atmospheric narrative (NPC dialogue + scene) only. No main story.

**Visual anchor:** 国朝墨彩 (Imperial Ink & Color) — Song dynasty court painting + ink wash. Explicitly Song dynasty aesthetic reference in both GDD and art bible (宋代院体画).

**Music references:** 《少年游》《破阵子》— both Song dynasty ci poems (词). 编钟 chime bells, 木鱼 wood blocks, 丝弦 silk strings.

**Protagonist:** 兔子武侠 — rabbit martial artist. Pillar 3 explicitly forbids replacing with human character as cultural differentiation. No name, backstory, or mythological connection specified.

**World gaps (as of first review):**
- No dynasty anchor stated (aesthetic implies Song, but never declared)
- No narrative frame for WHY there is a labyrinth / what the court is
- No lore for why combat is beat-quantized (in-world metaphysics absent)
- No boss names, types, or cultural identities
- No faction or political world-building
- 玉兔 (Jade Rabbit) mythological connection to Chang'e / Moon Rabbit unused and unaddressed

**Why:** First narrative review requested 2026-07-13 to identify authenticity gaps before world-building work begins.
**How to apply:** Use these gaps to prioritize what narrative scaffolding is needed before writing dialogue, lore, or NPC content. Do not assume gaps are intentional design choices — surface them for user decision.
